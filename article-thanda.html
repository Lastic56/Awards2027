Growing Stronger Communities from the Ground Up: Thanda

Thanda — Best Rural Community Project

About this finalist

CategoryBest Rural Community Project
RepresentativeAngela Larkan
Edition2027 CSI Legacy Awards
All finalists

Thriving rural communities are built through more than short-term assistance. They need access to quality education, opportunities to develop practical skills and the resources to create sustainable livelihoods. Thanda is working towards this vision by supporting children, their households and their wider communities through an integrated approach centred on education, skills development and farming. Its commitment to strengthening rural resilience has earned the organisation recognition as a finalist in the Best Rural Community Project category of the 2027 CSI Legacy Awards.

Thanda's work is rooted in the belief that rural communities already possess significant strengths, knowledge and potential. Rather than imposing solutions from the outside, the organisation seeks to build on these existing assets, working alongside communities to develop opportunities that respond to their needs and create lasting change. Its ultimate aim is to help break the cycle of poverty by supporting people to become more resilient, independent and equipped to shape their own futures.

The community development initiative recognised through the awards nomination brings together three interconnected areas: education, skills development and farming. These focus areas are designed to support people at different stages of life, recognising that a child's development is closely linked to the circumstances of their household and the opportunities available within their community.

Education is an important starting point. By investing in children's early learning and development, Thanda helps establish the foundations for future educational achievement. Its early childhood development (ECD) programmes provide support during the formative years, when access to stimulating learning experiences can make a significant difference to a child's readiness for formal schooling.

The results demonstrate the value of this investment. One particularly encouraging measure is that 84% of children participating across Thanda's ECD programmes are on track to start Grade R, compared with a national figure of 42%. This is an important indication of the progress that can be achieved when children receive the support and learning opportunities they need during their earliest years.

However, Thanda recognises that education alone cannot address the wider challenges facing rural households. Skills development and farming form an equally important part of its approach, helping individuals and families strengthen their capacity to generate income, improve food security and build more sustainable livelihoods. By connecting these areas, the organisation seeks to create benefits that extend beyond individual participants and contribute to the resilience of entire neighbourhoods.

At the heart of this work is Angela Larkan, whose commitment is reflected in the organisation's focus on practical, community-led change. For Larkan, the motivation comes from seeing the difference that sustained support can make in people's lives. The smiling faces of children, fields of growing vegetables and personal stories of individuals whose circumstances have changed are among the moments that make the work especially meaningful.

The progress of children within the ECD programmes is a particularly powerful source of encouragement. The finding that 84% are on track for Grade R represents more than a statistic; it reflects the efforts of practitioners, families and communities working together to give children a stronger start. It also demonstrates how targeted investment in early learning can help address educational inequalities before they become more difficult to overcome.

Being named a finalist in the 2027 CSI Legacy Awards is therefore a meaningful achievement for Thanda. For Larkan, the nomination represents recognition not only of the organisation's work, but also of the dedication of its staff and the communities who contribute to its success. It is an acknowledgement that lasting progress is built through collective effort and a shared commitment to creating opportunities.

The recognition also provides an opportunity to inspire other organisations and communities to take action. Thanda hopes its approach will demonstrate that meaningful change does not always begin with large-scale interventions, but with listening, collaboration and building on the strengths that already exist.

Through education, skills development and farming, Thanda is helping rural communities create the conditions in which children can thrive, households can become more resilient and opportunities can grow. Its work is a reminder that lasting development is not simply about providing support, but about empowering people to build a stronger future together.
