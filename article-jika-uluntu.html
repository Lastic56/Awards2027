Voices of Ubuntu: Jika Uluntu

Jika Uluntu NPC — Best Rising NGO

About this finalist

CategoryBest Rising NGO
RepresentativeZiyanda Claire Xaso
Edition2027 CSI Legacy Awards
All finalists

Jika Uluntu NPC, an Eastern Cape-based non-profit organisation dedicated to strengthening children, families and communities through culturally rooted approaches to social development, has been named a finalist in the 2027 CSI Legacy Awards in the Best Rising NGO category. The recognition celebrates the organisation's innovative work in child protection, emotional wellbeing and community development, as well as its commitment to creating lasting change through the power of African storytelling and creative expression.

Serving children, young people, caregivers and communities across the Eastern Cape, Jika Uluntu believes that meaningful social change begins by listening to communities and recognising the knowledge, experiences and cultural resources they already possess. Its work is grounded in the philosophy of Ubuntu, which emphasises humanity, interconnectedness and the importance of relationships in building stronger societies.

At the centre of its work is Voices of Ubuntu, a programme developed in response to the experiences of children and families in the Eastern Cape, alongside research exploring rural women's experiences and youth substance use. The organisation recognised that violence, poverty and unresolved trauma can leave children feeling unheard, place strain on family relationships and create barriers to accessing appropriate support. Too often, interventions fail to reflect the languages, cultures and lived experiences of the communities they aim to serve.

Voices of Ubuntu addresses these challenges through culturally rooted storytelling, puppetry, theatre, music and movement. Rather than relying exclusively on conventional educational approaches, the programme uses creative expression to help participants explore difficult experiences, develop emotional literacy and recognise healthier ways of responding to challenges. Through interactive activities, children and young people are encouraged to express themselves, build confidence, strengthen their sense of identity and develop the skills to navigate relationships more positively.

Importantly, the programme also engages caregivers and wider communities, creating opportunities for adults to reflect on their own experiences and understand the circumstances behind children's behaviour. By encouraging open conversations and shared learning, Voices of Ubuntu seeks to strengthen protective relationships, promote safer responses to harm and equip families to support children's emotional and social development. Its broader ambition is to build protective families, inclusive communities and young people who are confident in their ability to lead positive change.

Driving this work is Ziyanda Xaso, whose passion for child protection and community development is rooted in the belief that no child should have to carry pain in silence. She is particularly committed to creating environments where children feel heard, adults are encouraged to reflect on their own experiences, and families can practise healthier ways of relating to one another. She also recognises the importance of African languages, stories and creative traditions as powerful tools for healing, learning and strengthening community connections.

A particularly distinctive aspect of Jika Uluntu's model is its social enterprise approach. Through commissioned programmes delivered to schools, workplaces and institutions, the organisation creates paid professional opportunities for local artists and facilitators. This model not only recognises artists as skilled practitioners whose work can contribute meaningfully to social wellbeing, but also generates income to support the organisation's continued delivery and extend services to communities that may not otherwise be able to afford them.

The organisation's journey has been marked by several significant milestones. In 2022, Xaso received the Daily Dispatch Local Hero Award in recognition of her community contribution. In 2025, six young participants performed at the National Arts Festival, providing an important platform for their creative talents. Other milestones include presenting No Child Stands Alone at the 2026 provincial Day of the African Child and launching Journey to Self, demonstrating how the arts can create opportunities to address difficult subjects and encourage meaningful dialogue.

For Xaso and the Jika Uluntu team, being recognised in the 2027 CSI Legacy Awards is an opportunity to bring greater visibility to work that began within local communities. It also represents an opportunity to build partnerships, secure commissioned projects and attract funding that can support artists' livelihoods while widening access to Voices of Ubuntu.

Ultimately, Jika Uluntu's nomination celebrates more than a programme. It recognises an approach to social development that places children, families, culture and community participation at its centre. Through the power of creative expression and the principles of Ubuntu, the organisation is working towards a future in which children are protected, communities are strengthened and local talent is recognised as an essential part of sustainable social change.
