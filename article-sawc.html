Investing in People to Protect Nature: Southern African Wildlife College

Southern African Wildlife College (SAWC) — Best Green Impact Initiative

About this finalist

CategoryBest Green Impact Initiative
RepresentativeTrenika Rajcoomar
Edition2027 CSI Legacy Awards
All finalists

Africa's conservation challenges extend far beyond protecting wildlife and preserving natural habitats. They are closely connected to the livelihoods, opportunities and well-being of the communities living alongside protected areas. The Southern African Wildlife College (SAWC) recognises that lasting conservation depends on people and nature thriving together. Through its commitment to specialised conservation education, practical training and community development, the College has been named a finalist in the Best Green Impact Initiative category of the 2027 CSI Legacy Awards.

The Southern African Wildlife College delivers relevant conservation education, training and skills development to individuals and communities across South Africa and beyond. Its approach combines classroom learning with practical experience, using its unique location to simulate the complex conservation and community landscapes that graduates will encounter in the field. This applied learning environment allows students to develop the knowledge and capabilities needed to respond to real-world conservation challenges.

At the heart of the College's work is the understanding that environmental protection cannot be separated from socio-economic development. Many communities living near protected areas face unemployment, limited access to quality education and restricted opportunities to participate in the growing nature-based economy. These inequalities can create tensions around natural resources and undermine long-term conservation efforts. SAWC seeks to address these challenges by equipping people with accredited skills, supporting employment and entrepreneurship, and creating pathways towards career progression and sustainable livelihoods.

The initiative submitted for the awards reflects this integrated approach to conservation and community development. Rather than viewing conservation as an isolated environmental activity, it recognises the importance of addressing the social and economic circumstances that influence how people interact with their natural surroundings. Through education and skills development, the College works to reduce exclusion, strengthen local capacity and create opportunities for communities to benefit from the natural assets around them.

Its work also recognises the importance of environmental justice, cultural identity and indigenous knowledge. By supporting local participation and developing skills relevant to conservation and nature-based industries, SAWC helps communities become active contributors to decisions and activities that affect their environments. The intended impact is mutually reinforcing: stronger communities can support healthier ecosystems, while thriving natural environments can create opportunities for sustainable economic development.

For Trenika Rajcoomar, the purpose behind this work is rooted in the belief that lasting conservation is only possible when people and nature flourish together. Rajcoomar is particularly inspired by the College's ability to turn this principle into practical opportunities, giving individuals the knowledge, skills and confidence to become agents of change. The transformative power of education, community empowerment and sustainable livelihoods remains central to this commitment.

A significant part of Rajcoomar's contribution has involved strengthening the College's ability to demonstrate and communicate the impact of its work. One important milestone has been the development and advancement of its Monitoring, Evaluation, Reporting and Learning (MERL) framework. This provides a foundation for more systematic impact measurement, evidence-based decision-making and accountability, helping the organisation understand what is working, identify areas for improvement and demonstrate the value of its programmes.

Building relationships with corporate partners and growing support through Corporate Social Investment (CSI) have also been important priorities. These partnerships connect SAWC's conservation and community development initiatives with organisations seeking meaningful, measurable social and environmental impact. For Rajcoomar, seeing these relationships translate into stronger support, new opportunities and more sustainable outcomes has been particularly rewarding.

Being named a finalist in the 2027 CSI Legacy Awards is an opportunity to recognise the collective contribution of SAWC's staff, partners, donors and the communities it serves. It also highlights the importance of conservation education as a catalyst for wider social change.

Ultimately, the Southern African Wildlife College is working towards a future in which protecting Africa's natural heritage and improving people's lives are not competing priorities, but complementary goals. By investing in people, building skills and creating opportunities, the College is helping develop the next generation of conservation leaders while strengthening the communities that are essential to the long-term protection of the continent's biodiversity.
